Ghosts of the Great Highway

Mark Kozelek’s first album as Sun Kil Moon is slow and wistful, and it tells stories of boxers who died young, childhood memories, and empty American places. It moves between hushed nylon-string songs like “Carry Me Ohio” and loud, Crazy Horse-style electric pieces like “Salvador Sanchez.” That range keeps it from blurring into one long mood. The centerpiece is the 14-minute “Duk Koo Kim,” a patient, sad tribute to the South Korean fighter who died after a 1982 title fight. Kozelek’s warm baritone and his eye for small details make these songs about other people’s tragedies feel personal.
